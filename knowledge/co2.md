Title: CO₂ Levels — Overview and Impacts

Healthy indoor CO₂ levels indicate adequate ventilation and fresh air exchange. Persistently elevated CO₂ often signals under‑ventilation and can affect thinking speed, decision‑making, and comfort. As occupancy rises or outdoor air is restricted, indoor CO₂ tends to climb.

What elevated CO₂ suggests
- Ventilation may not match occupancy or schedule.
- Recirculation or closed dampers limit fresh air.
- Spaces with poor air distribution or blocked diffusers can accumulate exhaled air.

What occupants may experience
- Heavier, stale air; reduced alertness; headaches and drowsiness.
- Meeting rooms and classrooms can feel stuffy late in sessions.

Operational responses
- Use demand‑controlled ventilation to increase outdoor air when CO₂ rises.
- Check economizer and damper operation; verify airflow balance.
- Align ventilation schedules with actual occupancy patterns.
